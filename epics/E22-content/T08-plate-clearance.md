## Why it is built this way

**One image at a time, because the test is about a person and not about a book.** `SPEC.md` §8 states
the correction and names the trap it produces: Jordan (d. 1931) and Evermann (d. 1932) clear the test,
*but the artists are a separate question the first draft simply missed*. A work is not cleared; an
**illustrator** is. So clearance is per image: find the credit line, identify the person, find the
death year, apply the term, record all of it. A block of forty plates from one monograph is forty
determinations, and the one that is a staff illustrator with no credit line is the one that would have
shipped.

**A6 already implements the test; this task supplies the determinations and closes the coverage gap.**
E04/T06 landed `termFor(deathYear) => deathYear <= 1987 ? 80 : 70` and `clearToBundle`, the three
rejected illustrator strings, the ledger-field checks and the `origin: originated` path. What it could
not land is the other direction: A6 checks that every plate **in** the ledger is clear, not that every
species that **needs** a plate has one. `lonja-icons-and-plates` rule 7 makes a plate mandatory on any
result or account for a protected species or either member of a look-alike pair, and enforces it with
an `assert` in the art resolver — which fires at runtime, on a device, for a species nobody tested.
Moving that to a build assertion is the point of this task: **a protected species with no cleared
plate must not be shippable at all.**

**Where clearance fails, the answer is a commissioned plate, not a silhouette.**
`engraved-plates.md`: *a smudge of outline cannot separate two emperors, and the fisher carries the
fine*. T07's A14 already makes it impossible to grow a silhouette into a plate — eight subpaths, no
hatching. So a protected species whose historical plate cannot be cleared gets an originated one:
`origin: originated`, `illustratorDeathYear: 9999` as the sentinel, `licence:
commissioned-work-for-hire` with the agreement id, and the full ledger row, because S17 renders it.
That is a cost, and it is the cost the drop rule is supposed to impose.

**Unattributable means deleted, and the word "pending" appears nowhere.**
`catchlaw-content-pipeline` rule 8 and `licence-provenance.md`'s drop rules: no identified illustrator
→ drop the row; illustrator identified but death year unknown → drop, *an unknown death year is not an
early one*; a scan with no credit line → drop or commission; cleared under the US rule alone → drop
and re-clear. A6 already fails on `unknown`, `unidentified` and `TBD`, which are the same three
strings `check_content_pipeline.sh` check 3 greps for. What this task adds is discipline in the ledger
itself: a dropped plate leaves `species.plate_asset` NULL (§7.1 makes it nullable for exactly this),
and the reason it was dropped is recorded in `content/AUTHORING.md`'s clearance log — so the next
person does not spend an afternoon re-researching a plate somebody already rejected.

**The four look-alike pairs come with an instruction about what to draw.**
`engraved-plates.md` tabulates them and the character each plate must show: `lethrinus_nebulosus` vs
`lethrinus_lentjan` (blue spangles, cheek stripe, red opercular margin on *lentjan*),
`epinephelus_coioides` vs `epinephelus_malabaricus` (orange spot density, caudal blotch field),
`scomberomorus_commerson` vs `scomberomorus_guttatus` (vertical bar count and continuity versus
discrete spots), `venerupis_corrugata` vs `ruditapes_decussatus` (concentric versus crossed sculpture,
siphon separation). A pair whose two plates do not draw the distinguishing character is two pictures
of the same fish, which is worse than one picture — it makes a fisher confident about a
misidentification. The `difference_key` on the `lookalike` row is the text; the drawing is the
artist's brief; the coverage assertion is what makes both mandatory.

**Publication year stays in the ledger as evidence and is never compared to a threshold.**
`check_content_pipeline.sh` check 7 greps for `published_year < 1930` and its variants across Dart and
YAML, and E04/T06's case 18 already greps the package's own source. `source_year` remains a required
field — it is how a reviewer finds the original and confirms the plate was not traced from a modern
field guide (`engraved-plates.md`) — and it is never the test.

**Where the plate file lives at runtime is the same gap T07 recorded.** `SPEC.md` §8 bundles ~25 MB
under `assets/plate/`; `icon-system.md` puts authored plate SVG in `assets_src/` and generates
`plate_specs.g.dart`. This task authors at `app/assets/plate/` per §8 and does not re-argue it; the
decision belongs to E10 and to `DECISIONS.md`, and the ledger — which is the part that matters legally
— is identical either way.

## Implementation outline

1. `PlateCoverageAssertion` — collect the protected species set from the rule rows (`is_protected`)
   and the look-alike members from `lookalikes.yaml`; for each, require a `plate_asset` naming a
   cleared block. Report at the species row's line, because that is where the author is looking.
2. Reciprocity and `difference_key` checks on `lookalikes.yaml`, both as `A6` so a pair's failures
   arrive together.
3. Register beside the existing A6 rather than as a new id: `licence-provenance.md` and
   `build-assertions.md` both key plates to A6, and splitting the id would make the published failure
   shape wrong.
4. **Then clear the plates, one at a time.** For each candidate image: find the credit line, identify
   the illustrator as a person, find the death year, apply `termFor`, and record the eleven ledger
   fields with `cleared_on` and `cleared_by`. Bloch, Cuvier, Valenciennes and Day are candidates
   because §8 and the skill name them; each still needs its own determination, because a plate in a
   book by one of them may have been drawn by somebody else.
5. Drop anything that fails, and write the reason in the clearance log in `content/AUTHORING.md` —
   *not* in `plates.yaml` as a commented-out block, which is a pending state wearing a comment.
6. Commission originated plates for every protected species and every look-alike member left
   uncovered, with the pair's distinguishing character in the brief.
7. Regenerate `content/ATTRIBUTIONS/plates.md`; confirm E18 has a section to render.

## Definition of done

`CONVENTIONS.md` §8 applies in full, plus:

- [ ] All 18 rows pass, and each failed first except cases 9–12, whose vacuity at authoring time is
      stated in the commit body and resolved by the first cleared plate.
- [ ] 100 % branch coverage on the A6 coverage extension, including the ordinary-species path that
      must not fire.
- [ ] Every protected species and both members of all four look-alike pairs resolve to a cleared
      plate; no such species carries a NULL `plate_asset`.
- [ ] Every ledger row carries the eleven fields, including `cleared_on` and `cleared_by`; no row says
      `unknown`, `unidentified`, `TBD`, `pending` or `review: later`.
- [ ] Every `public_domain` plate names an illustrator who died in 1945 or earlier for a 2026 build;
      every `originated` plate carries the `9999` sentinel and its agreement id.
- [ ] `app/assets/plate/` is under 25 MB and every filename is a `lower_snake_case` binomial.
- [ ] `content/ATTRIBUTIONS/plates.md` regenerates with no diff and lists every plate's illustrator
      and death year.
- [ ] The clearance log in `content/AUTHORING.md` records every **rejected** image and why, so the
      research is not repeated.
- [ ] No publication-year comparison exists anywhere in `tools/content_builder/lib` or `content/`.
