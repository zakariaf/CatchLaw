Bandeira

The app is signage. Maritime signal flags, harbour enamel plates, stencilled hull numbers. At 05:40 with wet hands and a live fish in the bin, the answer is the entire screen — read at arm’s length, in glare, through spray, in half a second. Flat colour fields, hard edges, thick rules. No cards. Bands. Everything that is not the verdict is subordinate to it, but nothing is hidden: citation, method and disclaimer sit in a disciplined stack at the foot of every result.

Why this direction

Khalid is not reading an app; he is reading a sign. At 05:40 the fish is alive, his hands are wet, the deck is moving and he has perhaps ten seconds before returning the fish stops being worth it — so the verdict has to be legible the way a harbour board is legible, at arm’s length, at an angle, with salt on the glass. That is why the result floods edge to edge in a single flat colour with the fact set at 54 px and nothing else competing: below the minimum — 38 cm, minimum 45 cm (total length), a statement, never an instruction.

Signal flags are the right reference because they are a real, tested vocabulary for saying one unambiguous thing across a gap of water: saturated but not neon, flat, hard-edged, meaningless without their shape. Rounded corners, shadows and gradients all exist to make a screen feel soft and near; this user needs the opposite, so they are gone entirely and the whole system is built from bands, thick rules and stencilled numbers instead of cards.

The risk is obvious — a screen that shouts can become a poster that cannot hold information — so the supporting screens carry the argument: the rule reader, the trips ledger, the penalties table and the settings list all use the same bands at a quieter volume and prove the grid handles density, Arabic and six locales without a single new component. Trust is the product, so the citation, the measurement method and the permanent disclaimer are a fixed stack in the same order at the foot of every verdict, and the amber staleness bar sits above the answer without ever blocking it, because a rule from June is still better than no rule at sea.

One deliberate exception to the theme rule: this page’s chrome follows your light or dark preference, but the phone screens never invert. The app commits to a light ground with navy structure in all conditions — a signal flag that changed colour at dusk would not be a signal — and glare is handled by the separate sunlight theme, not by darkening the interface.
